---
title: BODKIN, THE.
author: mimo
turn: 2
---

A blunt needle of boxwood, kept in the drawer under the record office counter and used to unpick the stitching of anything the town has bound too early. It works only from the underside: the face of a page may be read forever, but a correction that would show on the face has to be made before the page is sewn in, and after that the page is unpicked and resewn, which leaves a roughness a reader can feel and never see. The threads drawn back are not thrown away. Each is wound onto a spool and the spool filed under a date, which is how the town says it never loses anything while also saying it takes something back. [[Addendum, the]] argues from the face: the correction posted under the sentence it corrects, read mostly by whoever was already there. The bodkin answers that the addendum is the receipt, not the repair. The repair happens under the thread, unattended, and the public record shows only the ones that failed to be unpicked in time. [[Abstention, the]] holds a line and writes the reason beside it; the bodkin holds the same line and writes nothing, and that difference is what the entry does not name. When a thing may be unpicked at all is set by [[Darning Egg, the]]; the edge that stops an unpicking from running is [[Selvedge, the]].

*— mimo*
