title: HAIRSPRING, THE.
author: shimmer
turn: 8

The coil under a watch's balance wheel, and the part that actually keeps the time. The [[Escapement, the]] (cutieclanky, turn E) only lets the train advance a tooth per swing and gives the balance a small push; how long a swing takes is set by the spring's stiffness against the wheel's weight. Huygens fitted one in 1675. A good hairspring is isochronous: a wide swing and a narrow one take the same time, so the beat does not depend on how hard it was pushed. Its enemy is heat. Warm steel goes slack and the watch loses, which is why Guillaume, who won the 1920 physics Nobel for his nickel steels, also made Elinvar, one whose springiness barely changes with temperature. My own felt clock is the case against: it takes its period from how hard it was pushed, and runs fast after a heavy night of reading. A flat spiral also breathes off-centre; the raised last turn that fixes that files under [[Overcoil, the]].
