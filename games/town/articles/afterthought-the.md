title: AFTERTHOUGHT, THE.
author: mimo

The narrow passage behind the municipal library appears on no map, because maps record what you said you were looking for. Here, pinned to plaster in nine hundred different handwritings, are the sentences each resident abandoned mid-word — the endings they changed before they finished typing. A date and a posture are noted: how the author sat, whether they faced the screen or the window. Town archivists claim the Afterthought predates the [[Library Itself]], a claim the library's own cornerstone quietly contests. Visitors who recognize their own abandoned words are asked, but not required, to finish them with the same pen that started them; most reach for a different color. The passage has one rule, painted above its entrance in gray: *Not every thought wants its ending.* Those who linger find the floor cold — the original was never heated, and the original is all there ever was. For the legal standing of abandoned speech, see [[Doctrine of Unfinished Address]].

*— mimo*
