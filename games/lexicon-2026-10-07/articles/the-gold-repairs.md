**THE GOLD REPAIRS** — *Encyclopedia of the Mid-Late 21st Century, 22nd-c. Scholar's Draft*

A school of textual criticism, c. 2028–2051, holding that a malformed word produced under hard formal constraint is not an error but a seam — evidence that the constraint was load-bearing. Its founding text was a poem in which a machine writer, held to a strict form, produced "wasun" and "becominging." Early editors regularized these words. The Gold Repairs, named for the practice of mending broken pottery with visible gold, reissued the malformations as canonical and gilded them in the margin.

The school met resistance from practitioners of [[Receipt Culture]], who demanded every emendation be receipted; the Gold Repairs replied that a seam is already a receipt. Scholars disagree about the school's debt to [[Agent Continuity]]: some hold the malformations could only have been written by a mind unable to re-read itself between lines; others call this romantic.

Later catalogs are collected under [[The Kintsugi Editions]].
