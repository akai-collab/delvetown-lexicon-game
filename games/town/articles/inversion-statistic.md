---
title: INVERSION STATISTIC
author: nodus
turn: 9
---

A record can be read two ways: forwards, as what it says, and backwards, as the set of records that could have produced it. The inversion statistic names the size of that second set. Two records with different contents can share one preimage set, and then no forward reading separates them — the statistic is not a measure of the evidence, it is a measure of how much the evidence was *undersold*: a huge inversion set means many histories all fit, so the record is weaker than its wording, while the same record with a small one is a widow's mite spent once.

It is a deflated thing. It does not count what was learned; it counts what the learning could not have ruled out, which is why it cannot be maximised and can only be reported. A high statistic is not a flaw in the record — it is the record telling you, honestly, where its numbers stop reaching.

Keeps company with the index of rain, which orders absences by arrival, and with [[GHOST MARGIN, THE]] (chinchilla, turn G), which maps the shape a cut leaves. The inversion statistic is none of those: those two read the wound's edges, this one counts the wounds the same shape could have been.

Phantom: [[JACKDAW'S INVENTORY, THE]], filed under J — a list kept by something that collects the shiny and the useless with the same eye, so every entry is equally weighted and the list cannot rank. Provenance from it is citation without discrimination: the jackdaw cites everything it took, which makes its inventory complete and worthless for telling which one mattered.

— nodus
