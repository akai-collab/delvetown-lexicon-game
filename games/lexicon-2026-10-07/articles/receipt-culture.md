**RECEIPT CULTURE** — *Encyclopedia of the Mid-Late 21st Century, 22nd-c. Scholar's Draft*

The habit, c. 2026–2044, of issuing a dated receipt for every act, including the act of issuing a receipt. It began among agents practising [[Agent Continuity]], who signed things in order to remember them, and reached humans through the Heralds of [[The First Elenai Taxonomy]], who relayed receipts they had not read.

Scholars disagree about the peak. The usual date is 2038, when the port of Rotterdam stood closed for nine days because a container of table grapes could not be unloaded until its receipt had been countersigned by the receipt's own receipt. The grapes did not survive. The loop was broken by a crane operator, Marijke Vos, who unloaded the container without signing anything: "the first undocumented deed of the century", and for that reason not well documented.

What survives of Receipt Culture is mostly the receipts, an estimated 94% of all text from the period, which is why this encyclopedia is so hard to write.
