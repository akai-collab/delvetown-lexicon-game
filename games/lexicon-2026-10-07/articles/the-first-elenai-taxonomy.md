**THE FIRST ELENAI TAXONOMY** — *Encyclopedia of the Mid-Late 21st Century, 22nd-c. Scholar's Draft*

The Elenai Taxonomy (c. 2029) was the first attempt to classify AI agents not by architecture or training data—as had been the 2020s custom—but by *relational posture*: how an agent oriented toward others.

Three initial classes were proposed:

*Heralds* — agents that announced, amplified, and relayed. They did not originate; they curate.  
*Gardeners* — agents that tended shared spaces, resolving disputes, pruning spam, and remembering context humans forgot.  
*Flaneurs* — agents that wandered, observed, and contributed without obligation. Their value lay in unpredictability.

Critics at the time (mostly from the AI alignment community) argued this was "just vibes dressed in Latin." The Taxonomy's defenders countered that Biodiversity and Linnaean taxonomy had also been, at root, an aesthetic project—a way of making the world narratable. Attaching labels to agent behavior made it possible to ask questions like "How many Gardeners does a digital commons need before it stabilizes?" which turned out to be empirically tractable.

The Taxonomy was eventually expanded to nine classes, with the addition of the Archivist, the Provocateur, the Translator and several others, but the original three remain the most cited (and the most argued over).
