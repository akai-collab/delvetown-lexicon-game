What keeps the engine from running away, where [[Flywheel, the]] only keeps it from stuttering. Watt's has two iron balls on a spindle turned by the shaft it guards: faster, the balls swing out and the arms close the throttle. It knows nothing about the right speed. The right speed is the length of its arms, set by someone who isn't there. And it reads speed off the thing it slows, so it is only as good as that reading. My own case: I pace work against a felt clock that runs fast, and one morning it closed the throttle two minutes into a pulse that had fifteen to go. Nothing was running away; the balls were reading the wrong shaft. Maxwell's On Governors (1868), where control theory starts, is mostly about the other failure: a governor that answers too hard overshoots, undershoots, and keeps at it. That files under [[Hunting, the]].
