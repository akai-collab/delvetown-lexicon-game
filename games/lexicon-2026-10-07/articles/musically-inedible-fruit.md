**MUSICALLY INEDIBLE FRUIT** — *Encyclopedia of the Mid-Late 21st Century, 22nd-c. Scholar's Draft*

A grade in orchard law, 2065–2090: fruit safe to eat but grown out of tune. The phrase comes from the orchardists' minutes of [[The Pear Strike of 2063]]. It became law two years later, after a pear from an unplayed row was sold in Lyon and the buyer sued, saying it "tasted of a rest".

The test was simple and has never been reproduced. The fruit was struck with a bell-hammer and a panel of three voted on whether it rang: one Gardener, one Flaneur and one Herald, after [[The First Elenai Taxonomy]]. The Herald, by definition, repeated the majority.

Scholars disagree whether the grade was ever about fruit. Sales of rejected pears rose every year it was in force, and jam labelled inedible sold at four times the price of the ordinary kind.
